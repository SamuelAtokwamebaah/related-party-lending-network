## 1. Hub detection — does anyone connect more than one failed institution?

**Degree centrality** counts how many connections a node has. **Betweenness centrality** counts how often it lies on the shortest path between two other nodes — the broker measure, and the one that would identify a person or company bridging separate failures.

**Betweenness is zero for all but 17 of the 63 nodes** (highest 0.0185). That is not a finding about Ghana's savings and loans sector; it is a property of a graph in seventeen pieces. A node inside a three-node component can lie between almost nothing, so the measure has nothing to measure. A betweenness ranking drawn from this graph would be a number presented as an insight.

Degree is meaningful within a component, and is reported:

## 2. Closed loops — does ownership or lending fold back on itself?

A **cycle** is a path returning to where it began. In a related-party structure it is the signature worth looking for: value leaves an institution and control over it returns, or two institutions fund each other. Direction matters, so this runs on the directed graph.

**Cycles found: 4. Of those, closed extraction loops — where every leg moves value: 1.**

- FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited
    - FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited: `proposed_merger_with` — **does not move value**
    - Ideal Finance Limited → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited: `related_party_exposure_to` — moves value

- Liberty Asset Management → Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited → Liberty Asset Management
    - Liberty Asset Management → Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited: `placed_funds_with` — moves value
    - Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited → Liberty Asset Management: `over_exposed_to` — moves value

- Ideal Capital Partners → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited → Ideal Capital Partners
    - Ideal Capital Partners → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited: `placed_funds_with` — moves value
    - FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited: `proposed_merger_with` — **does not move value**
    - Ideal Finance Limited → Ideal Capital Partners: `related_party_exposure_to` — moves value

- Ideal Capital Partners → Ideal Financial Holdings → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited → Ideal Capital Partners
    - Ideal Capital Partners → Ideal Financial Holdings: `placed_funds_with` — moves value
    - Ideal Financial Holdings → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited: `acquired` — **does not move value**
    - FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited: `proposed_merger_with` — **does not move value**
    - Ideal Finance Limited → Ideal Capital Partners: `related_party_exposure_to` — moves value

The loops marked as moving value on every leg are the structural signature of related-party extraction and warrant a case walkthrough of their own.

## 3. Community detection — do clusters share owners or counterparties?

**Community detection** looks for groups more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the graph. **Modularity** scores how well a division separates it, from 0 (no better than random) towards 1.

- components: 17
- communities detected: 17
- modularity: 0.8955

**The communities detected are exactly the connected components.** The algorithm found nothing the component structure had not already given, which is what happens on a disconnected graph: there is no denser-than-average grouping to find inside a three-node star.

The modularity of 0.8955 is high, and presenting it as a strong result would be the easiest mistake available with this dataset. Modularity is high *because* the graph is fragmented — a partition into disconnected pieces always scores well, since no edge crosses between them. Here it measures the fragmentation, not the discovery of community structure. It is recorded so the mistake is not made later.

## 6. The placement layer, and why it is kept out of the figures above

The Securities and Exchange Commission notice describes a second kind of relationship that the Bank of Ghana notice does not: a fund manager placing client money in one of the 23, with no related-party claim attached. Value moves. No insider relationship is alleged.

There are **13 such edges**, from **7 fund managers**, reaching **8 of the 23**. They are held in a separate layer, marked `placement` in `relationship_types`, filtered out of `v_institution_profile`, and excluded from every figure in sections 0 to 5.

**What that separation is worth, as a number.** Five of the 7 fund managers name more than one of the 23. Admitting these as ordinary edges would take the graph from **17 components, largest 10**, to **12 components, largest 26** — a single structure holding most of the graph. That structure would be real, but it would not be an insider network. It would be a set of institutions that happen to share a fund manager who placed client money in both, which is a different fact and a weaker one.

|---|---|
| FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited | Goldstreet Fund Management Limited, Integrity Fund Management Ltd, Procap Finance Limited |
| Legacy Capital Savings and Loans Ltd. | Integrity Fund Management Ltd, Monarch Capital Limited |
| Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited | Integrity Fund Management Ltd, Procap Finance Limited, Standard Securities Limited |

The one institution here that the Bank of Ghana did **not** flag for related-party exposure is Commerz Savings and Loans Limited. Section 4's comparison is therefore a statement about the Bank of Ghana notice, not about the record as a whole.

## What this section does not claim

- The related-party graph is not shown to be one network. It is 17 separate structures, and only one entity — Ideal Capital Partners — connects two of the 23, on the Securities and Exchange Commission's notice. The Bank of Ghana notice alone names no such entity, which is what section 1 reported before that notice was read.
- Betweenness centrality and community detection return nothing interpretable on a graph in seventeen pieces, and the high modularity score measures that fragmentation rather than any discovered structure.
- The placement layer is not evidence of an insider relationship, and no figure above treats it as one.
- Amounts are not comparable across institutions. Some are exact, some are floors, some are totals shared between counterparties, one transfer is stated in three currencies, and the Commission's figures are percentages of a fund's own book rather than cedi sums. Only exact cedi amounts on value-extracting edges are summed anywhere above; nothing sums a percentage.
- Nothing here is an allegation. Each edge restates what a regulator published, and the passage behind it is quoted in `data/evidence.csv` with a line anchor into the source.
- GN Savings and Loans is excluded from the worked examples. The Court of Appeal quashed the revocation of its licence on 21 May 2026 and the appeal is pending; its edges remain, still attributed to the Bank of Ghana.
