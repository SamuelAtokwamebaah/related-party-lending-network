## 1. Hub detection: does anyone connect more than one failed institution?

**Degree centrality** counts how many connections a node has. **Betweenness centrality** counts how often it lies on the shortest path between two other nodes. That is the broker measure, and the one that would identify a person or company bridging separate failures.

**Betweenness is zero for all but 16 of the 64 nodes** (highest 0.0179). That is not a finding about Ghana's savings and loans sector; it is a property of a graph in seventeen pieces. A node inside a three-node component can lie between almost nothing, so the measure has nothing to measure. A betweenness ranking drawn from this graph would be a number presented as an insight.

Degree is meaningful within a component, and is reported:

## 2. Closed loops: does ownership or lending fold back on itself?

A **cycle** is a path returning to where it began. In a related-party structure it is the signature worth looking for: value leaves an institution and control over it returns, or two institutions fund each other. Direction matters, so this runs on the directed graph.

**Cycles found: 5. Of those, closed loops where every leg moves value: 1.**

These are named *circular funding* rather than *extraction* loops, which is the weaker and more accurate word. A leg marked as moving value says the relationship moves money; it does not say the money left one of the 23. In the one case below it moves both ways: out of the institution to a related party, and back in from that party's client funds. That is circular funding between related parties. Whether either side ended up better off is a question these documents do not answer.

- Crest Finance House Limited → UNNAMED parent company of Crest → Crest Finance House Limited
    - Crest Finance House Limited → UNNAMED parent company of Crest: `over_exposed_to`, moves value
    - UNNAMED parent company of Crest → Crest Finance House Limited: `owns`, **does not move value**

- FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited
    - FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited: `proposed_merger_with`, **does not move value**
    - Ideal Finance Limited → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited: `related_party_exposure_to`, moves value

- Liberty Asset Management → Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited → Liberty Asset Management
    - Liberty Asset Management → Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited: `placed_funds_with`, moves value
    - Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited → Liberty Asset Management: `over_exposed_to`, moves value

- Ideal Capital Partners → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited → Ideal Capital Partners
    - Ideal Capital Partners → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited: `placed_funds_with`, moves value
    - FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited: `proposed_merger_with`, **does not move value**

- Ideal Capital Partners → Ideal Financial Holdings → FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited → Ideal Finance Limited → Ideal Capital Partners

The loops marked as moving value on every leg are the circular funding described above, and each has a case walkthrough of its own.

## 3. Community detection: do clusters share owners or counterparties?

**Community detection** looks for groups more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the graph. **Modularity** scores how well a division separates it, from 0 (no better than random) towards 1.

- components: 17
- communities detected: 17
- modularity: 0.8966

**The communities detected are exactly the connected components.** The algorithm found nothing the component structure had not already given, which is what happens on a disconnected graph: there is no denser-than-average grouping to find inside a three-node star.

The modularity of 0.8966 is high, and presenting it as a strong result would be the easiest mistake available with this dataset. Modularity is high *because* the graph is fragmented. A partition into disconnected pieces always scores well, since no edge crosses between them. Here it measures the fragmentation, not the discovery of community structure. It is recorded so the mistake is not made later.

**The groups separate cleanly.** Every institution carrying a value-moving edge out is one of the flagged 17, and none outside them carries one.

**This is a consistency check, not a confirmation, and the distinction matters.** The flag comes from `ghana-banking-collapse`, which coded it from the Bank of Ghana notice of 16 August 2019. The edges were extracted from the same notice. Two readings of one document agreeing is evidence that the extraction pipeline neither dropped an institution nor invented one, which is worth knowing and is why it is reported, but it is not a second source corroborating the first. A perfect separation here was close to guaranteed by where both variables come from, and it should not be read as the graph validating the published count.

## 6. The placement layer, and why it is kept out of the figures above

The Securities and Exchange Commission notice describes a second kind of relationship that the Bank of Ghana notice does not: a fund manager placing client money in one of the 23, with no related-party claim attached. Value moves. No insider relationship is alleged.

There are **13 such edges**, from **7 fund managers**, reaching **8 of the 23**. They are held in a separate layer, marked `placement` in `relationship_types`, filtered out of `v_institution_profile`, and excluded from every figure in sections 0 to 5.

**What that separation is worth, as a number.** Five of the seven fund managers name more than one of the 23. Admitting these as ordinary edges would take the graph from **17 components, largest 10**, to **12 components, largest 26**, a single structure holding most of the graph. That structure would be real, but it would not be an insider network. It would be a set of institutions that happen to share a fund manager who placed client money in both, which is a different fact and a weaker one.

| institution | fund managers placing client funds there |
|---|---|
| FirsTrust Savings and Loans Limited | Goldstreet Fund Management Limited, Integrity Fund Management Ltd, Procap Finance Limited |
| Legacy Capital Savings and Loans Ltd. | Integrity Fund Management Ltd, Monarch Capital Limited |
| Midland Savings and Loans Company Limited | Integrity Fund Management Ltd, Procap Finance Limited, Standard Securities Limited |

Of the 8 institutions this layer reaches, the table lists those with more than one fund manager. The one the Bank of Ghana did **not** flag for related-party exposure is Commerz Savings and Loans Limited. Section 4's comparison is therefore a statement about the Bank of Ghana notice, not about the record as a whole.

## What this section does not claim

- The related-party graph is not shown to be one network. It is 17 separate structures, and only one entity, Ideal Capital Partners, connects two of the 23, and only when both notices are read: the Bank of Ghana records one institution's exposure to it, and the Securities and Exchange Commission records it placing funds with another. Neither notice alone names such an entity, which is what section 1 reported before the second was read.
- Betweenness centrality and community detection return nothing interpretable on a graph in 17 pieces, and the high modularity score measures that fragmentation rather than any discovered structure.
- The placement layer is not evidence of an insider relationship, and no figure above treats it as one.
- Amounts are not comparable across institutions. Some are exact, some are floors, some are totals shared between counterparties, one transfer is stated in three currencies, and the Commission's figures are percentages of a fund's own book rather than cedi sums. Only exact cedi amounts on value-moving edges are summed anywhere above; nothing sums a percentage.
- Nothing here is an allegation. Each edge restates what a regulator or, in one case, a court published, and the passage behind it is quoted in `data/evidence.csv` with a line anchor into the source.
- GN Savings and Loans is excluded from the worked examples. The Court of Appeal quashed the revocation of its licence on 21 May 2026, and the Supreme Court stayed that judgment on 14 July 2026 pending appeal. Its Bank of Ghana edges remain, attributed to the Bank; its membership of Groupe Nduom is attributed to the Court of Appeal.
