Seventeen structures, not one network

Related-party lending appears in 17 of the 23 savings & loans companies and finance houses whose licences the Bank of Ghana revoked on 16 August 2019. This is what those relationships look like when the published notice is read as a graph — and what it turns out not to show.

What the record supports

The project set out to find the people and companies sitting behind more than one failure. The public record does not contain them. Each named counterparty, holding company and unnamed related party in the notice attaches to exactly one institution, so the graph falls into 17 disconnected pieces rather than forming a single insider network.

That is a finding about the source, not about the sector. The revocation notice was written to explain why each institution failed, never to map ownership across them. Establishing whether the same people recur needs company-registry or court records, which the repository’s limitations set out.

The two documented networks

The only structures in the record with more than four entities. Both are worth reading edge by edge.

Ideal Financial Holdings, Ideal Finance and FirsTrust

A common owner above two separately licensed institutions that lent to each other. Ideal Financial Holdings acquired EZI Savings and Loans in 2014 and renamed it FirsTrust. Ideal Finance then carried related-party exposure to FirsTrust and to Ideal Capital Partners. In June 2019 the two applied to merge and the Bank of Ghana declined, both being insolvent.

GN Savings and Loans within the Groupe Nduom network

The largest quantified structure in the notice. GN placed GHS 761.55 million with two sister companies licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission rather than the Bank of Ghana, and a further transfer went to a company the notice states is owned by the same group and based in the United States.
