Seventeen structures, not one network

Related-party lending appears in 17 of the 23 savings & loans companies and finance houses whose licences the Bank of Ghana revoked on 16 August 2019. This is what those relationships look like when two regulators’ notices are read as a graph, including one loop that neither document shows on its own.

What one regulator sees, and what two see

Read on its own, the Bank of Ghana’s notice shows no entity connecting two of the 23 institutions. Every counterparty it names attaches to exactly one. That is a fact about the document: it was written to explain why each institution failed, not to map ownership across them.

Adding the Securities and Exchange Commission’s notice of 8 November 2019, which revoked 53 fund-management licences, changes that. The Commission states what the banking regulator had no reason to: where fund managers had placed their clients’ money. Eleven of its 53 sections name one of the 23 institutions.

The clearest result needs both documents and appears in neither alone. The Bank of Ghana records Midland as over-exposed to Liberty Asset Management; the SEC records Liberty holding 9.45% of its funds with Midland. Each held the other, and value moves along both arrows: the one closed funding loop in the evidence base. Money leaves Midland to a related party, and that party’s client funds come back in. Whether either side ended better off is not something these documents say.

The graph is still in 17 pieces, and one entity now connects two institutions where none did before. A negative result from a single source is a statement about that source.

The documented networks

The largest structures in the record, each worth reading edge by edge. A component containing a contested institution is not shown here as a case study.

uniCredit Ghana, uniSecurities and the Hoda companies

The largest structure in the evidence base, and it takes both regulators to see it. The Bank of Ghana records that uniCredit, formerly Kantamanto Savings and Loans, was acquired by the Hoda Group of Companies in 2006, and that it was over-exposed to its sister company uniSecurities to the tune of GHS 160.10 million. The SEC then lists seven related parties of Unisecurities (Ghana) Limited, the first of which is Hoda Holdings, under a finding about its placement of client funds; it does not say how much went to each. That the SEC's Unisecurities (Ghana) Limited is the Bank of Ghana's uniSecurities rests on the name, and the limitations say so. Whether Hoda Holdings and the Hoda Group are one entity is an open question in this repository, not an assumption: if they are, the ownership closes back on itself.

Ideal Financial Holdings, Ideal Finance, FirsTrust and the group companies

Two licensed institutions and the group companies around them. The Bank of Ghana records that Ideal Financial Holdings acquired EZI Savings and Loans in 2014, which became FirsTrust, and that Ideal Finance carried related-party exposure to FirsTrust and to Ideal Capital Partners. The two institutions sought to merge in 2019, by a letter of 25 June and a request dated 25 July, and the Bank of Ghana refused, both being insolvent. The SEC adds what the banking regulator had no reason to record: Ideal Capital Partners had 19% of its funds under management in related parties, among them the holding company, FirsTrust, and two non-financial group companies, Ideal Trade and Ideal Bottling. It is the only entity in the evidence base that connects two of the 23, and the connection needs both notices. That Ideal Financial Holdings also owns Ideal Finance is inferred from the shared name alone, and is not drawn.

Midland and Liberty Asset Management

The smallest of the three and the most complete. The Bank of Ghana records Midland as over-exposed to related parties including Liberty Asset Management; the SEC records Liberty holding 9.45% of its funds with Midland, and 10.93% with Liberty Commodities. Each institution held the other, and value moves along both arrows. It is the only cycle here in which every leg moves value, and neither regulator’s notice shows it alone. Circular funding between related parties is what the documents establish; calling it extraction would claim a direction they do not.

The other 14

Each is an institution and the related parties the notices, and in one case a court, attribute to it. None connects to any other panel, and that disconnection is the finding, not a drawing artefact.

What was left out, and why

A separate finding, kept out of every number above.

The Commission’s notice describes a second kind of relationship the Bank of Ghana’s does not: a fund manager placing client money in one of the 23, with no related-party claim attached. Value moves. No insider relationship is alleged. There are 13 such edges, from 7 fund managers, reaching 8 of the 23.

Admitting them as ordinary edges would take the graph from 17 components, largest 10, to 12 components, largest 26, one structure holding most of the graph. That structure is real. It is not an insider network: it is a set of institutions that happen to share a fund manager who placed client money in both. Presenting it as the headline would be the most flattering version of this project and the least true one.

How value moved out of the institutions

Every edge carries a relationship from a controlled vocabulary. On this evidence base the mechanism counts carry more than any network measure does.

Counted here are only the 34 relationships that move value out of one of the 23. A further 17 move value along a related-party relationship that runs the other way, or between two parties neither of which is one of the 23, mostly fund managers placing their clients’ money with their own related companies. That is a real finding and a different one, and adding it here would make this chart say more than it means.

Built from two regulators’ notices: the Bank of Ghana’s of 16 August 2019 revoking the licences of 23 savings and loans companies and finance houses, and the Securities and Exchange Commission’s of 8 November 2019 revoking 53 fund-management licences. One edge, GN Savings and Loans’ membership of Groupe Nduom, comes from the Court of Appeal judgment of 21 May 2026, which the Supreme Court has stayed pending appeal. Every node and edge carries a citation to a line in its source; the quoted passages are in the repository’s evidence base. Inferred relationships are excluded from this page.

What this cannot show is written down. No individual appears as a node in this graph, the graph is in 17 pieces, and most relationships carry no figure at all. The limitations are set out in full in docs/LIMITATIONS.md, and are worth reading before the diagrams rather than after them.

Nothing here is an allegation. Each relationship restates what a regulator or a court published, and the page takes no position on any proceeding. Evidence base v0.7, all 75 edges verified against source.
